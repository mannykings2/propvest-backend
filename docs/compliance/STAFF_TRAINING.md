# PropVest AML/CFT Staff Training Note

**Status:** Signed-off training record — owner: Compliance Officer
**Audience:** every PropVest staff member (admin panel access or not)
**Frequency:** onboarding + annually; refreshers after any rule or procedure
change

---

## 1. Why you are reading this

Every PropVest employee is part of the AML/CFT programme. Regulators judge the
programme by what *staff* did, not by what the software did. This note is the
10-minute version; the full rules live in the
[AML/CFT Policy](AML_CFT_POLICY.md) and [STR Procedure](STR_PROCEDURE.md).

## 2. The five things every staff member must know

1. **The 24-hour STR clock.** Once anyone determines a transaction is
   suspicious, the report to NFIU is due within **24 hours**. The Risk panel
   shows the countdown; red means the deadline is gone.
2. **No tipping off.** Never tell a customer their account is under AML
   review, that a report was filed, or anything from which they could infer
   it. This is a criminal offence, not a guideline.
3. **KYC is not rubber-stamping.** Approval requires your own sanctions/PEP
   check recorded as *clear*. A hit blocks approval, freezes the account and
   escalates automatically.
4. **Never clear an alert without notes.** The system refuses it because the
   note *is* the evidence that a human actually reviewed the pattern.
5. **Audit logs remember everything.** Every admin action — KYC view, decision,
   screening, alert toggle, STR filing, account flag — is recorded with who,
   when, from where. Acting suspiciously yourself will be visible.

## 3. Red flags (non-exhaustive)

- Several deposits just under a reporting threshold, then one withdrawal
  (structuring)
- New account funded high and drained within days (mule behaviour)
- Withdrawals to an account whose name differs from the customer's KYC name
- One funding source paying into several unrelated wallets
- Round-amount cycling (the same ₦ figure moved repeatedly)
- A customer unusually interested in limits, thresholds or "what gets
  reported"
- Staff exception requests ("just this once", "override for this customer")

## 4. What to do when you see a red flag

1. Do not confront the customer and do not change anything informally.
2. Note the facts (user, transactions, times) and report to the Compliance
   Officer.
3. If you have admin access: take the alert into review, add your notes; the
   Compliance Officer decides escalation.
4. If you believe a colleague is involved, go straight to the CEO.
